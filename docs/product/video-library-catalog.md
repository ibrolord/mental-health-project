# MHtoolkit Video Library Catalog

Status: research shortlist for the planned books-and-videos library. This file
does not add videos to the product by itself.

Last link check: 2026-07-28. All 35 official source URLs returned HTTP 200 after
redirects.

## Selection Standard

- Use the seven existing library topics so books and videos can share one
  filter, search, save, note, and action model.
- Link to the publisher's official page. Do not download, rehost, or copy a
  third-party transcript.
- Treat every talk as education or personal perspective, not diagnosis,
  treatment, medical advice, or a guaranteed outcome.
- Give each item a clear premise, practical takeaway, and an optional next step
  that can become a journal note, goal, or habit.
- Show a content note before trauma, grief, abuse, death, or mature sexual
  content. Never autoplay sensitive content.
- Preserve user choice. A user can skip a talk, dismiss a recommendation, or
  save it without creating a goal.
- Review provider usage terms before embedding video. The safe first release is
  a link to the official source in the user's browser.

## Proposed Product Fields

The later unified content model should support:

- `id`
- `mediaType`: `book` or `video`
- `topic`
- `title`
- `creator`
- `provider`
- `sourceUrl`
- `centralPremise`
- `practicalTakeaways`
- `reflectionPrompt`
- `suggestedAction`
- `contentNote`
- `editorialCaveat`
- `saved`, `priority`, `customNotes`, and optional journal, goal, or habit links

The order below is the recommended display order within each topic.

## Anxiety & stress

### 1. How to make stress your friend

- **ID:** `video-anxiety-stress-friend`
- **Speaker / source:** Kelly McGonigal, TED
- **Official link:** https://www.ted.com/talks/kelly_mcgonigal_how_to_make_stress_your_friend
- **Premise:** Changing how a person interprets some stress responses may make
  those responses feel more manageable, while social connection can be an
  important part of coping.
- **Takeaways:** Name the body signal without immediately treating it as danger;
  identify one person or resource that can reduce isolation.
- **Platform integration:** Journal prompt: "What is this stress response trying
  to help me prepare for?" Suggested action: contact one safe person or take one
  concrete step toward the stressor.
- **Editorial caveat:** Reframing is not a cure for chronic, traumatic, unsafe,
  or medically significant stress and should not be used to tolerate harm.

### 2. The gift and power of emotional courage

- **ID:** `video-anxiety-emotional-courage`
- **Speaker / source:** Susan David, TED
- **Official link:** https://www.ted.com/talks/susan_david_the_gift_and_power_of_emotional_courage
- **Premise:** Difficult emotions can carry useful information when they are
  acknowledged without letting them dictate every action.
- **Takeaways:** Use precise language for an emotion; choose a next action based
  on values rather than short-term avoidance.
- **Platform integration:** Journal prompt: "What am I feeling, and what value
  does this feeling point toward?" Suggested action: create one value-aligned
  goal with a very small first step.
- **Editorial caveat:** Accepting an emotion does not mean accepting abuse,
  danger, discrimination, or an unhealthy environment.

### 3. Why you should define your fears instead of your goals

- **ID:** `video-anxiety-fear-setting`
- **Speaker / source:** Tim Ferriss, TED
- **Official link:** https://www.ted.com/talks/tim_ferriss_why_you_should_define_your_fears_instead_of_your_goals
- **Premise:** A structured review of feared outcomes, prevention, repair, and
  the cost of inaction can make an avoided decision more concrete.
- **Takeaways:** Separate likely risks from vague dread; write one prevention or
  repair step for the most credible risk.
- **Platform integration:** Journal prompt: "What could happen, how could I
  reduce the risk, and how could I recover?" Suggested action: save one
  time-boxed decision step as a goal.
- **Editorial caveat:** Fear-setting can intensify rumination for some people.
  Keep the exercise short, stop if distress escalates, and seek appropriate
  support for high-stakes or safety-related decisions.

### 4. All it takes is 10 mindful minutes

- **ID:** `video-anxiety-mindful-minutes`
- **Speaker / source:** Andy Puddicombe, TED
- **Official link:** https://www.ted.com/talks/andy_puddicombe_all_it_takes_is_10_mindful_minutes
- **Premise:** Brief, regular periods of observing the present moment can create
  space between an experience and an automatic reaction.
- **Takeaways:** Start with a short practice rather than an ideal routine; notice
  thoughts without trying to eliminate them.
- **Platform integration:** Journal prompt: "What did I notice without needing
  to fix it?" Suggested habit: two minutes of optional present-moment attention,
  increasing only if it feels useful.
- **Editorial caveat:** Mindfulness is not calming for everyone and can be
  activating for some trauma survivors. Offer grounding alternatives and an
  immediate stop control.

### 5. 3 ways to overcome anxiety

- **ID:** `video-anxiety-three-strategies`
- **Speaker / source:** Olivia Remes, TEDx
- **Official link:** https://www.ted.com/talks/olivia_remes_3_ways_to_overcome_anxiety
- **Premise:** Small changes in how a person approaches uncertainty, effort, and
  self-talk may reduce avoidance and build a sense of agency.
- **Takeaways:** Choose a "good enough" first attempt; respond to mistakes with
  the same fairness offered to another person.
- **Platform integration:** Journal prompt: "What would a good-enough attempt
  look like today?" Suggested action: create one deliberately small exposure or
  practice step.
- **Editorial caveat:** This is a short educational TEDx talk, not an anxiety
  assessment or treatment plan. Persistent or severe symptoms need qualified
  care.

## Mood & self-compassion

### 1. Why we all need to practice emotional first aid

- **ID:** `video-mood-emotional-first-aid`
- **Speaker / source:** Guy Winch, TED
- **Official link:** https://www.ted.com/talks/guy_winch_why_we_all_need_to_practice_emotional_first_aid
- **Premise:** Rejection, loneliness, failure, and rumination deserve deliberate
  care rather than dismissal simply because the injury is emotional.
- **Takeaways:** Notice repetitive self-criticism; interrupt one unhelpful loop
  with a grounded, compassionate response.
- **Platform integration:** Journal prompt: "What emotional injury needs care,
  not judgment?" Suggested action: save one coping response that has helped
  before.
- **Editorial caveat:** "Emotional first aid" is a metaphor, not crisis care,
  diagnosis, or a substitute for professional support.

### 2. The power of vulnerability

- **ID:** `video-mood-vulnerability`
- **Speaker / source:** Brene Brown, TEDx
- **Official link:** https://www.ted.com/talks/brene_brown_the_power_of_vulnerability
- **Premise:** Meaningful connection often requires tolerating uncertainty and
  being seen without controlling every outcome.
- **Takeaways:** Distinguish honest sharing from oversharing; identify one
  trustworthy context where a small honest statement is appropriate.
- **Platform integration:** Journal prompt: "Where would safe, bounded honesty
  help me feel less alone?" Suggested action: plan one low-risk conversation
  with a trusted person.
- **Editorial caveat:** Vulnerability should be earned and context-sensitive. It
  is not an instruction to disclose to unsafe, coercive, or untrustworthy people.

### 3. The surprising science of happiness

- **ID:** `video-mood-adaptation`
- **Speaker / source:** Dan Gilbert, TED
- **Official link:** https://www.ted.com/talks/dan_gilbert_the_surprising_science_of_happiness
- **Correction link:** https://blog.ted.com/ten-years-later-dan-gilbert-on-life-after-the-surprising-science-of-happiness/
- **Premise:** People can adapt to outcomes in ways they do not predict well,
  and wellbeing is not limited to getting a preferred result.
- **Takeaways:** Treat a forecast about future happiness as uncertain; identify
  one source of agency available after an unwanted outcome.
- **Platform integration:** Journal prompt: "Which prediction am I treating as a
  certainty?" Suggested action: record an alternative path or coping resource.
- **Editorial caveat:** TED later published corrections to factual claims in the
  original talk. Keep the broad reflection on adaptation, link the correction,
  and do not repeat corrected claims as settled evidence.

### 4. What makes a good life? Lessons from the longest study on happiness

- **ID:** `video-mood-good-life`
- **Speaker / source:** Robert Waldinger, TEDx
- **Official link:** https://www.ted.com/talks/robert_waldinger_what_makes_a_good_life_lessons_from_the_longest_study_on_happiness
- **Premise:** The quality of supportive relationships is strongly associated
  with health and wellbeing across time.
- **Takeaways:** Invest in relationship quality rather than contact count;
  repair or strengthen one connection through a specific action.
- **Platform integration:** Journal prompt: "Which relationship feels
  nourishing, strained, or neglected?" Suggested action: create one connection
  habit, such as a weekly check-in.
- **Editorial caveat:** Long-running observational research does not provide one
  universal recipe. Culture, access, safety, disability, and individual
  circumstances affect how connection is built.

### 5. There's more to life than being happy

- **ID:** `video-mood-meaning`
- **Speaker / source:** Emily Esfahani Smith, TED
- **Official link:** https://www.ted.com/talks/emily_esfahani_smith_there_s_more_to_life_than_being_happy
- **Premise:** Belonging, purpose, coherent life stories, and moments of
  transcendence can support meaning even when constant happiness is unrealistic.
- **Takeaways:** Identify which source of meaning is undernourished; choose one
  contribution or connection that matters independent of mood.
- **Platform integration:** Journal prompt: "What still matters on a difficult
  day?" Suggested action: create one purpose-linked goal or belonging habit.
- **Editorial caveat:** A meaning framework is not a treatment for depression
  and should not pressure people to turn pain into a lesson.

## Habits & growth

### 1. The power of believing that you can improve

- **ID:** `video-habits-growth-mindset`
- **Speaker / source:** Carol Dweck, TEDx
- **Official link:** https://www.ted.com/talks/carol_dweck_the_power_of_believing_that_you_can_improve
- **Premise:** Treating ability as developable can make feedback, effort, and
  strategy changes easier to use.
- **Takeaways:** Replace a global verdict with a specific skill gap; ask what
  strategy, support, or practice would improve the next attempt.
- **Platform integration:** Journal prompt: "What am I still learning?" Suggested
  action: create one process goal based on strategy rather than outcome.
- **Editorial caveat:** Growth mindset is not "effort fixes everything."
  Resources, teaching quality, health, disability, opportunity, and structural
  conditions also matter.

### 2. Grit: The power of passion and perseverance

- **ID:** `video-habits-grit`
- **Speaker / source:** Angela Duckworth, TED
- **Official link:** https://www.ted.com/talks/angela_lee_duckworth_grit_the_power_of_passion_and_perseverance
- **Premise:** Sustained effort toward a meaningful long-term direction can
  matter alongside talent.
- **Takeaways:** Clarify whether a goal still deserves persistence; define a
  repeatable practice instead of relying on motivation.
- **Platform integration:** Journal prompt: "Should I persist, change strategy,
  pause, or stop?" Suggested habit: one small practice tied to a chosen goal.
- **Editorial caveat:** Grit is not the sole cause of success. Rest, resources,
  safety, discrimination, changing priorities, and the option to stop all matter.

### 3. How to get better at the things you care about

- **ID:** `video-habits-learning-zone`
- **Speaker / source:** Eduardo Briceno, TED
- **Official link:** https://www.ted.com/talks/eduardo_briceno_how_to_get_better_at_the_things_you_care_about
- **Premise:** Deliberate time in a learning zone, where mistakes are expected
  and feedback is used, differs from time spent only performing.
- **Takeaways:** Separate practice from evaluation; choose one skill and one
  feedback signal before starting.
- **Platform integration:** Journal prompt: "What can I practice where mistakes
  are allowed?" Suggested habit: a short weekly learning-zone session.
- **Editorial caveat:** A learning zone requires enough safety, time, access, and
  feedback. Not every workplace or life context provides those conditions.

### 4. Try something new for 30 days

- **ID:** `video-habits-thirty-day-experiment`
- **Speaker / source:** Matt Cutts, TED
- **Official link:** https://www.ted.com/talks/matt_cutts_try_something_new_for_30_days
- **Premise:** A short, bounded experiment can make a desired change easier to
  begin and evaluate.
- **Takeaways:** Make the experiment observable and small; review what happened
  rather than treating a missed day as failure.
- **Platform integration:** Journal prompt: "What did this experiment teach me?"
  Suggested habit: a user-selected trial with flexible frequency and an end
  review.
- **Editorial caveat:** Thirty days is a convenient experiment length, not a
  scientific guarantee that a behavior will become automatic.

### 5. The surprising habits of original thinkers

- **ID:** `video-habits-original-thinkers`
- **Speaker / source:** Adam Grant, TED
- **Official link:** https://www.ted.com/talks/adam_grant_the_surprising_habits_of_original_thinkers
- **Premise:** Original work often includes doubt, delayed incubation, many weak
  ideas, and repeated attempts rather than instant certainty.
- **Takeaways:** Generate several options before judging them; define a
  reversible test for one idea.
- **Platform integration:** Journal prompt: "What are five possible approaches,
  including imperfect ones?" Suggested goal: run one bounded experiment and
  record what was learned.
- **Editorial caveat:** Failure should not be romanticized. Keep experiments
  proportional, reversible, and safe.

## Burnout & recovery

### 1. The cure for burnout (hint: it isn't self-care)

- **ID:** `video-burnout-stress-cycle`
- **Speaker / source:** Emily Nagoski and Amelia Nagoski, TED
- **Official link:** https://www.ted.com/talks/emily_nagoski_and_amelia_nagoski_the_cure_for_burnout_hint_it_isn_t_self_care
- **Premise:** Stress responses and the conditions causing stress are related
  but distinct, and recovery includes helping the body move out of persistent
  threat activation.
- **Takeaways:** Distinguish the stressor from the stress response; choose a safe
  activity that helps the body shift state.
- **Platform integration:** Journal prompt: "What is the stressor, and what does
  my body need after it?" Suggested action: save one realistic recovery practice.
- **Editorial caveat:** Burnout is not merely an individual coping failure.
  Workload, control, fairness, support, caregiving, health, and systemic
  conditions may need to change.

### 2. How to turn off work thoughts during your free time

- **ID:** `video-burnout-work-rumination`
- **Speaker / source:** Guy Winch, TED
- **Official link:** https://www.ted.com/talks/guy_winch_how_to_turn_off_work_thoughts_during_your_free_time
- **Premise:** Persistent work rumination can consume recovery time, and clear
  boundaries plus concrete closure practices can reduce mental carryover.
- **Takeaways:** Write down the next action before stopping; create a physical or
  temporal transition out of work.
- **Platform integration:** Journal prompt: "What remains unresolved, and what
  is the next scheduled action?" Suggested habit: a two-minute work shutdown
  note.
- **Editorial caveat:** A shutdown routine cannot repair an unsafe, exploitative,
  or chronically overloaded work environment.

### 3. The real reason why we are tired and what to do about it

- **ID:** `video-burnout-types-of-rest`
- **Speaker / source:** Saundra Dalton-Smith, TEDx
- **Official link:** https://www.ted.com/talks/saundra_dalton_smith_the_real_reason_why_we_are_tired_and_what_to_do_about_it
- **Premise:** Feeling depleted may involve unmet physical, mental, sensory,
  creative, emotional, social, or spiritual needs rather than sleep alone.
- **Takeaways:** Name the kind of depletion instead of using only "tired";
  choose one matching form of rest.
- **Platform integration:** Journal prompt: "Which kind of rest feels missing?"
  Suggested action: add one short, category-matched recovery activity.
- **Editorial caveat:** The seven-types framework is an educational model, not a
  medical diagnosis. Persistent fatigue can have medical or psychiatric causes
  and deserves appropriate evaluation.

### 4. How to succeed? Get more sleep

- **ID:** `video-burnout-sleep`
- **Speaker / source:** Arianna Huffington, TED
- **Official link:** https://www.ted.com/talks/arianna_huffington_how_to_succeed_get_more_sleep
- **Premise:** Treating sleep deprivation as a badge of commitment undermines
  wellbeing and can impair judgment and performance.
- **Takeaways:** Notice where sleep is being traded for avoidable demands;
  choose one realistic wind-down or scheduling adjustment.
- **Platform integration:** Journal prompt: "What is one pressure competing with
  sleep?" Suggested habit: a user-defined wind-down cue without rigid streaks.
- **Editorial caveat:** This is a brief motivational talk, not insomnia
  treatment. Persistent sleep problems, breathing issues, or severe daytime
  sleepiness need qualified care.

### 5. In praise of slowness

- **ID:** `video-burnout-slowness`
- **Speaker / source:** Carl Honore, TED
- **Official link:** https://www.ted.com/talks/carl_honore_in_praise_of_slowness
- **Premise:** Deliberately matching pace to the activity can protect attention,
  quality, relationships, and recovery from a constant bias toward speed.
- **Takeaways:** Identify one task where rushing reduces quality; choose one
  transition or activity to do at a deliberate pace.
- **Platform integration:** Journal prompt: "Where is speed helping, and where is
  it costing me?" Suggested habit: one protected slow block per week.
- **Editorial caveat:** Slowing down is not equally available in every job,
  caregiving role, disability context, or financial situation.

## Trauma

Every item in this section needs a visible content note and must never autoplay.
The product should offer a neutral "Not now" action before opening the source.

### 1. How childhood trauma affects health across a lifetime

- **ID:** `video-trauma-aces-health`
- **Speaker / source:** Nadine Burke Harris, TED
- **Official link:** https://www.ted.com/talks/nadine_burke_harris_how_childhood_trauma_affects_health_across_a_lifetime
- **Premise:** Adverse childhood experiences are associated at a population
  level with later health risks, making trauma-informed prevention and care
  important.
- **Takeaways:** Replace blame with context; identify one current source of
  safety, support, or qualified care.
- **Platform integration:** Journal prompt: "What helps me feel safer in the
  present?" Suggested action: save a support resource, with no requirement to
  record trauma details.
- **Content note:** Childhood abuse, neglect, family adversity, illness, and
  mortality.
- **Editorial caveat:** An ACE score is not a diagnosis or destiny and should
  not be used by MHtoolkit to predict an individual's health.

### 2. A broken body isn't a broken person

- **ID:** `video-trauma-broken-body`
- **Speaker / source:** Janine Shepherd, TED
- **Official link:** https://www.ted.com/talks/janine_shepherd_a_broken_body_isn_t_a_broken_person
- **Premise:** A devastating injury can disrupt an expected identity without
  erasing personhood or the possibility of a different meaningful future.
- **Takeaways:** Separate identity from one lost role; name an ability, value, or
  relationship that remains available.
- **Platform integration:** Journal prompt: "What part of me remains larger than
  this loss?" Suggested action: define one adaptive, present-day goal.
- **Content note:** Severe accident, critical injury, disability, rehabilitation,
  and loss of an expected future.
- **Editorial caveat:** This is one person's recovery story, not a standard that
  injured or disabled people should be expected to match.

### 3. I am the son of a terrorist. Here's how I chose peace.

- **ID:** `video-trauma-chosen-values`
- **Speaker / source:** Zak Ebrahim, TED
- **Official link:** https://www.ted.com/talks/zak_ebrahim_i_am_the_son_of_a_terrorist_here_s_how_i_chose_peace
- **Premise:** A person can examine inherited beliefs and choose values that
  differ from a violent family or social environment.
- **Takeaways:** Identify one belief that deserves re-examination; choose one
  action that expresses a personally selected value.
- **Platform integration:** Journal prompt: "Which value is mine to choose?"
  Suggested goal: one safe action that reinforces that value.
- **Content note:** Terrorism, murder, family violence, hatred, and childhood
  fear.
- **Editorial caveat:** A story of agency is not trauma treatment and should not
  imply that recovery is achieved by willpower alone.

### 4. Our lonely society makes it hard to come home from war

- **ID:** `video-trauma-veteran-belonging`
- **Speaker / source:** Sebastian Junger, TED
- **Official link:** https://www.ted.com/talks/sebastian_junger_our_lonely_society_makes_it_hard_to_come_home_from_war
- **Premise:** Social belonging and the conditions of returning home may affect
  how some veterans experience readjustment after war.
- **Takeaways:** Consider belonging as part of recovery; identify one
  community-based source of practical connection.
- **Platform integration:** Journal prompt: "Where do I experience mutual
  belonging?" Suggested action: save one peer, veteran, cultural, or community
  resource.
- **Content note:** War, combat, post-traumatic stress, suicide, and social
  isolation.
- **Editorial caveat:** This is one social interpretation, not a complete
  account of PTSD or veteran experience and not a substitute for trauma-informed
  care.

### 5. How the worst moments in our lives make us who we are

- **ID:** `video-trauma-forging-meaning`
- **Speaker / source:** Andrew Solomon, TED
- **Official link:** https://www.ted.com/talks/andrew_solomon_how_the_worst_moments_in_our_lives_make_us_who_we_are
- **Premise:** People may construct identity and meaning in response to
  adversity, even when they did not choose or deserve what happened.
- **Takeaways:** Distinguish what happened from the identity built around it;
  choose whether meaning-making feels useful now.
- **Platform integration:** Journal prompt: "What meaning, if any, do I choose
  to carry forward?" Suggested action: save a self-defined value, with "skip"
  always available.
- **Content note:** Bullying, violence, rape, illness, disability, and identity
  conflict.
- **Editorial caveat:** No one owes meaning, growth, forgiveness, or gratitude
  for trauma.

## Grief & loss

Every item in this section needs a visible content note and must never autoplay.

### 1. We don't "move on" from grief. We move forward with it

- **ID:** `video-grief-moving-forward`
- **Speaker / source:** Nora McInerny, TED
- **Official link:** https://www.ted.com/talks/nora_mcinerny_we_don_t_move_on_from_grief_we_move_forward_with_it
- **Premise:** Continuing to live does not require erasing a person, relationship,

- **Takeaways:** Let continuing bonds coexist with change; reject a rigid

- **Platform integration:** Journal prompt: "What do I want to carry forward

  note or ritual.
- **Content note:** Death of a spouse, parent, and pregnancy loss, with candid
  humor about grief.
- **Editorial caveat:** Grief varies widely. The app should not score, stage, or
  prescribe a timeline for it.

### 2. 3 secrets of resilient people

- **ID:** `video-grief-resilient-people`
- **Speaker / source:** Lucy Hone, TEDx
- **Official link:** https://www.ted.com/talks/lucy_hone_3_secrets_of_resilient_people
- **Premise:** During severe adversity, people may benefit from recognizing
  suffering, directing attention toward available supports, and asking whether a
  behavior helps or harms.
- **Takeaways:** Notice one thing that remains supportive without denying pain;
  use "Is this helping me?" before a next choice.
- **Platform integration:** Journal prompt: "What hurts, and what is still
  supporting me?" Suggested action: record one supportive choice for today.
- **Content note:** Sudden death of a child and parental grief.
- **Editorial caveat:** Resilience is not constant strength, quick recovery, or
  a moral requirement. Support needs differ.

### 3. What really matters at the end of life

- **ID:** `video-grief-end-of-life`
- **Speaker / source:** BJ Miller, TED
- **Official link:** https://www.ted.com/talks/bj_miller_what_really_matters_at_the_end_of_life
- **Premise:** End-of-life care can center comfort, dignity, sensory experience,
  relationships, and the person's own priorities rather than medical treatment
  alone.
- **Takeaways:** Ask what matters to the person now; make room for comfort and
  human connection in serious-illness conversations.
- **Platform integration:** Journal prompt: "What gives this day dignity or
  meaning?" Suggested action: save one question for a care team or trusted
  person.
- **Content note:** Death, dying, hospice, serious illness, and severe injury.
- **Editorial caveat:** Medical, legal, cultural, and spiritual decisions belong
  with the person, their chosen supports, and qualified professionals.

### 4. Don't regret regret

- **ID:** `video-grief-regret`
- **Speaker / source:** Kathryn Schulz, TED
- **Official link:** https://www.ted.com/talks/kathryn_schulz_don_t_regret_regret
- **Premise:** Regret is a common human response that can reveal values and
  inform future choices without defining a person's worth.
- **Takeaways:** Name the value underneath a regret; separate a repairable action

- **Platform integration:** Journal prompt: "What does this regret show that I
  care about?" Suggested goal: one repair, learning, or self-forgiveness step.
- **Content note:** Personal mistakes, loss, shame, and irreversible decisions.
- **Editorial caveat:** Severe or persistent guilt, self-punishment, or suicidal
  thinking requires support beyond a reflection exercise.

### 5. How to fix a broken heart

- **ID:** `video-grief-broken-heart`
- **Speaker / source:** Guy Winch, TED
- **Official link:** https://www.ted.com/talks/guy_winch_how_to_fix_a_broken_heart
- **Premise:** Heartbreak can create powerful cognitive and behavioral loops,
  and recovery may involve reducing idealization, accepting available
  explanations, and rebuilding daily life.
- **Takeaways:** Balance idealized memories with a fuller account; deliberately
  refill one routine, social, or identity gap.
- **Platform integration:** Journal prompt: "What gap did this loss leave?"
  Suggested action: create one reconnection or routine-building step.
- **Content note:** Romantic separation, rejection, grief, and emotional pain.
- **Editorial caveat:** Safety, coercion, stalking, abuse, severe depression, or
  crisis needs appropriate support, not a breakup checklist.

## Relationships & boundaries

### 1. The difference between healthy and unhealthy love

- **ID:** `video-relationships-healthy-love`
- **Speaker / source:** Katie Hood, TED
- **Official link:** https://www.ted.com/talks/katie_hood_the_difference_between_healthy_and_unhealthy_love
- **Premise:** Patterns such as intensity, isolation, belittling, volatility, and
  possessiveness can help people recognize when a relationship is becoming
  unhealthy.
- **Takeaways:** Evaluate repeated patterns rather than one label; identify one
  trusted person or resource outside the relationship.
- **Platform integration:** Journal prompt: "Which patterns increase or reduce
  safety and respect?" Suggested action: save a private support resource rather
  than prompting confrontation.
- **Content note:** Emotional abuse, controlling behavior, and relationship
  violence.
- **Editorial caveat:** If abuse or coercion may be present, prioritize safety
  planning and qualified support. Do not recommend couples communication as the
  default response.

### 2. How to build (and rebuild) trust

- **ID:** `video-relationships-rebuild-trust`
- **Speaker / source:** Frances Frei, TED
- **Official link:** https://www.ted.com/talks/frances_frei_how_to_build_and_rebuild_trust
- **Premise:** Trust can be examined through whether people experience
  authenticity, sound judgment, and concern for them rather than treated as a
  vague all-or-nothing feeling.
- **Takeaways:** Name the specific trust dimension that feels weak; request one

- **Platform integration:** Journal prompt: "What evidence would make trust
